# Darkr for Apple Devices — Privacy Policy

**Effective date:** 28 October 2026
**Applies to:** the Darkr app and Safari extension for **macOS**, **iPhone** and **iPad**
**Developer:** joshmode
**See also:** the [general privacy policy](PRIVACY.md) for the Chrome extension.

Darkr is a reading utility for Safari that changes how web pages look — dark, sepia, grayscale and warm-light themes. It is built to work entirely on your device.

## Summary

- **Darkr collects no data.** Nothing about you or your browsing is collected, stored on a server, shared, sold or used for tracking.
- Darkr has **no account, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no network connection of its own.**
- Your settings stay on your device, inside Safari's local extension storage.

This matches Darkr's App Store privacy label: **Data Not Collected.**

## What the Darkr app does

The Darkr app is a small companion that helps you turn the Safari extension on. It:

- shows setup instructions and a preview of the reading modes,
- checks, using Apple's Safari APIs, **whether the Darkr extension is currently enabled**, and
- offers a shortcut to open Safari's settings.

The app does not ask for, read or store your personal information, and it does not connect to the internet.

## What the Safari extension does

When you enable Darkr in Safari, it can theme the websites you allow it to run on. It uses that access only to:

1. read the colors a page's own styles define (computed styles), and
2. apply a color theme by adding a style sheet and small Darkr-specific attributes to page elements.

Darkr **does not read, copy, store or transmit** page text, form entries, passwords, cookies, messages, photos or any other page content. It loads no remote code and contains no third-party code at runtime. Choosing *Original* mode, pausing Darkr, or removing the extension removes everything Darkr added to the page.

### Website access

Safari asks you which websites Darkr may run on, and you can change this at any time in **Safari → Settings → Extensions** (macOS) or **Settings → Apps → Safari → Extensions** (iPhone and iPad). Darkr only acts on sites you allow. It reads the address of the current tab only to show which site your toolbar choices apply to, and does not save it unless you create a site rule.

## What Darkr stores on your device

| Stored | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Appearance settings (mode, background tone, contrast, sepia, grayscale and warmth levels) | To apply your chosen look |
| Your on/off switch and how "Automatic" sites behave | To remember your choice |
| **Site rules**: website hostnames you set to *Always* or *Never*, or gave their own appearance, and whether to theme that site over its own dark mode | To apply per-site choices |
| A flag recording that you dismissed the first-run guidance | So it is shown once |

This information lives only in Safari's local extension storage on your device. Darkr does not upload it, and Darkr does not sync it. It is not collected by the developer. If you back up your device, your backup may include Safari's extension data under Apple's own privacy terms.

## Information handled by Apple

Darkr does not receive these, but you should know they exist:

- **App Store and TestFlight.** Apple processes your purchase or download under the Apple Privacy Policy.
- **Analytics sharing.** If you turned on *Share With App Developers* in your device's privacy settings, Apple may provide the developer with aggregated, anonymous usage and crash statistics. Darkr adds nothing to this and cannot identify you from it.
- **Safari.** Safari's own handling of your browsing is governed by Apple's policies, not Darkr's.

## Children

Darkr collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

## Your controls

- Change or clear settings any time in Darkr's Settings page (**Clear all site rules**, **Restore defaults**).
- Pause Darkr with the switch in the toolbar popup, or set a site to *Never*.
- Turn the extension off in Safari's settings, or delete the app, to remove it. Deleting the app removes the extension and its stored settings.
